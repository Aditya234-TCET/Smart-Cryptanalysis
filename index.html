Cryptographic Concept Hub & Interactive Study Guide

Caesar Cipher & Chi-Squared

The Caesar Cipher is a monoalphabetic substitution where each letter is shifted by a constant $k$. It is broken easily because the letter frequency shape is unchanged—simply shifted.

By computing the Chi-Squared ($\chi^2$) statistic for all 26 possible shifts against standard English frequencies, we can automatically determine the correct key: the shift with the minimum $\chi^2$ represents the perfect alignment.

Vigenère Cipher & IoC

The Vigenère Cipher employs a repeating keyword to select shifts dynamically, smoothing out the unigram letter frequencies. We crack this using the Index of Coincidence (IoC).

IoC measures letter distribution variance. A random sequence has IoC $\approx 0.038$, while English has IoC $\approx 0.0667$. By testing periods (key lengths) and evaluating coset IoCs, we locate the true key length because its cosets align monoalphabetically, boosting the IoC peak.

Monoalphabetic & Simulated Annealing

General Monoalphabetic Substitution maps each letter randomly (key space of $26! \approx 4 \times 10^{26}$). Since brute-force is impossible, we use a Heuristic Hill-Climbing / Simulated Annealing solver.

We swap keys and evaluate the decrypted text's Bigram Fitness Score (summing the log-probabilities of English letter pairings like TH or HE). Annealing allows accepting worse fits early to escape local maxima, slowly settling on the global maximum representing English plaintext.

Transpositions & Playfair Grid

Rail Fence is a transposition cipher (shuffling letters without replacing them). Since letter frequencies are unaffected, Chi-squared is low, but bigram and word-match scores drop. We brute-force rails and evaluate word counts to solve.

Playfair Cipher encrypts letters in pairs (digrams) on a 5x5 grid. This breaks single-letter frequency analysis but is vulnerable to digram frequency analysis because double letter splits and character rectangles show structural patterns.
